I keep a set of rules that fire before I break them.

Over the past months I distilled about thirty books — engineering, negotiation, typography, colour theory, brand economics, one chart-hacking manual from 1988, and one labor study of the adult-content industry — into decision rules. Software agents did the reading under a strict contract: every rule needs an observable trigger, a falsifiable claim, a question you can answer before you act, and a source. I did the keeping and the cutting. The cuts are documented in the repo with reasons, because curation you can't audit is just vibes.

A few that earn their keep:

"What behavior does this pay for at 2 a.m. with no witnesses?" — Munger, on every KPI and pricing scheme.

"Would you read 3,000 words in this face?" — the question that ends most brand-font debates.

"If the platform dies tomorrow, what residual remains?" — from workers who built durable income with no funding, no safety net, and no polite treatment.

The full set is public: github.com/darce/heuristics. Seven skills, ~160 rules, sources cited by chapter. They read as documents and load into coding agents as skill files.

I'll publish one rule a week for a while, each applied to a real decision from that week, building toward a launch in the accessibility space — where trust in AI output is the entire product. If a rule fails you in practice, file a falsification report in the repo. That is what they're for.

— Daniel
