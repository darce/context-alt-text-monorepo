## ch-1 — Definition: New Rich vs Deferrers {#ch-1}

**Core claim:** People don’t want to be millionaires—they want the **lifestyle** they believe only millions buy. The **New Rich (NR)** abandon the **deferred-life plan** and create luxury of time and place *now* via **Lifestyle Design (LD)**.

**Freedom multiplier:** money’s practical value scales with how many of the four W’s you control—**what, when, where, with whom**. An 80-hour, $500k banker can be “poorer” in lifestyle output than a $40k worker with full control of schedule and location.

| Deferrer (D) goal | New Rich (NR) reframing |
|---|---|
| Work for yourself | Have others work for you / own the trains |
| Work when you want | Minimum necessary for maximum effect (**minimum effective load**) |
| Retire early | **Mini-retirements** distributed through life; inactivity is not the goal |
| Buy things | Do and *be*; stuff is means or bonus |
| Be the boss | Be neither boss nor employee—**owner** |
| Make a ton of money | Money with defined dreams, timelines, steps |
| Reach the big payday | Cash flow first; big exit second |
| Freedom from dislike | Freedom *to* pursue excitement without work-for-work’s-sake (**W4W**) |

**Order of operations:** entrepreneurs implement **D-E-A-L**. Employees remaining employed implement **D-E-L-A**—**Liberation** (remote arrangement) before deep hour-cutting, because office culture punishes visible idleness even when output is high.

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Career plan assumes “work now, live at 65” | Treat retirement as worst-case insurance, not the prize |
| Comparing yourself by salary alone | Optimize **relative income** ($ per hour of *your* time), not absolute income |
| Job is “fine” but not improving | Wishful optimism often = fear of uncertainty; if no improvement trend, plan a jump |

↔ contra “save hard and retire rich” popularizations: deferral is the bug, not the feature when markets/health/politics can zero the end pot.

## ch-2 — Rules that change the rules {#ch-2}

**Beating the game, not playing it:** exploit official rules, not only self-imposed “how things are done.” Fosbury flop / kickboxing technicalities = test assumptions when status-quo results are subpar. Different is better only when more effective or more fun.

### Ten NR differentiators (keep names/phrasing)

1. **Retirement is worst-case-scenario insurance** — not the goal.
2. **Interest and energy are cyclical** — plan mini-retirements / work–recovery cadence.
3. **Less is not laziness** — productivity ≠ busy sacrifice theater.
4. **The timing is never right** — “someday” is a disease; act and course-correct.
5. **Ask for forgiveness, not permission** — if moderate/reversible damage, move first.
6. **Emphasize strengths, don’t fix weaknesses** — multiply weapons; don’t patch armor to mediocrity.
7. **Things in excess become their opposite** — idle excess is poison; free time = *chosen* activity.
8. **Money alone is not the solution** — money as scapegoat postpones self-examination.
9. **Relative income > absolute income** — same $50k at 10 hrs/week beats $50k at 80 hrs/week 4× on hourly basis (once absolute floor for dreams is met).
10. **Distress bad, eustress good** — avoid destructive stress; seek growth stress (training, role models, expandable comfort).

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Team optimizes optics of effort | Measure contribution by results, not hours |
| Waiting for “perfect conditions” | Ship the important move; timing never aligns |
| Fixing every weakness | Double-down on top strengths; outsource or ignore the rest |

## ch-3 — Fear-setting {#ch-3}

**Most people choose unhappiness over uncertainty.** **Fear-setting** (named procedure): define the nightmare in painful detail *before* the leap so vague anxiety becomes a repair plan.

### Procedure (do in writing; volume > polish)

1. Define absolute worst outcomes if you take the step. Permanent impact 1–10? Likelihood?
2. Steps to repair damage / regain baseline even temporarily.
3. More-probable benefits (internal + external), impact 1–10, likelihood.
4. Run the same on “fired tomorrow” and “quit to test options.”
5. Name what you postpone out of fear — *what we fear doing most is usually what we most need to do*.
6. Cost of **inaction** at 1 / 5 / 10 years (financial, emotional, physical).
7. If waiting only for “good timing,” you’re afraid—act.

**Risk definition used:** likelihood of an **irreversible** negative outcome. Temporary 3–4 pain vs permanent 9–10 upside is the usual asymmetric trade once nightmare is defined.

Scenario → lesson (≤6 lines):
- Lawyer Hans: paraglide panic → resign → surf company; colleagues say “throwing it all away” → later clients whisper they want the same → **lesson:** risks shrink once taken; deferred life is the real trap.
- Ferriss trip: six months of shame-loop → fear-setting → one-way ticket; business *improved* while he limited email to Mondays → **lesson:** removing the founder bottleneck often *raises* profit.

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Big reversible experiment blocked by vague dread | Run full fear-setting worksheet before deciding |
| “It will get better with time/money” with no evidence | Treat as optimistic denial; demand improvement metrics or plan exit |
| Inaction framed as prudence | Score irreversible downside of *staying* |

## ch-4 — System reset: dreamlining {#ch-4}

**Opposite of happiness is boredom**, not sadness. Chase **excitement**, not abstract “happiness.” Goal questions:

- Bad: “What do I want?” / “What are my goals?”
- Better: **“What would excite me?”**

**Adventure Deficit Disorder (ADD):** culture installs “be realistic” after school; entrepreneurs still fail when “make $X then live” never defines the *then*. Without alternate activities, X rises forever (**fat man in the red BMW** pattern).

### Dreamlining (named; timelines on dreams)

Not ordinary goal-setting: goals must be **unrealistic** to work; convert *being* → *doing*; fill the vacuum when work drops.

1. Two timelines (6 and 12 months): up to 5 each of **have / be / do**—no how yet; guilt-filter off.
2. If blocked: if you had $100M, what daily? What makes waking exciting? Fallback do-list: one place, one once-in-lifetime, one daily, one weekly, one learn.
3. Convert each “be” into an observable action (“great cook” → Christmas dinner unassisted).
4. Star **four** most exciting/important on 6-month line.
5. Cost those four as **monthly cash flow**; TMI = sum of dream monthly costs + (current monthly expenses × **1.3** buffer). Optional: one-time goals / months. TDI = TMI / 30.
6. Three steps per dream; **first step now** (≤5 minutes); next steps before 11am tomorrow and next day. Prefer call someone who’s done it over research paralysis.

**Unrealistic is easier:** fiercest competition sits on “realistic” goals. Big payoff fuels endurance through setbacks. Success ≈ number of uncomfortable conversations you’re willing to have.

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Goals are vague lifestyle adjectives | Dreamline with TMI math; first action same day |
| Only “having” goals | Require *do/be* so free time has content |
| Competing on mediocre targets | Raise the target until competition thins |

## ch-5 — Elimination: 80/20 and Parkinson’s {#ch-5}

**Forget time management.** Busy is often avoidance of few uncomfortable critical actions. **Effectiveness** = doing things that move goals. **Efficiency** without effectiveness is refined waste.

### Pareto procedure (80/20 Principle)

Minimum ratio to seek: **80/20** (often 90/10, 95/5, 99/1). Ask:

1. Which **20% of sources** cause **80% of problems/unhappiness**?
2. Which **20% of sources** produce **80% of desired outcomes/happiness**?

Apply to customers, products, ads, affiliates, friends, tasks. Then: fire/passivate the toxic low-yield; profile and **duplicate** top producers.

Scenario → lesson:
- 120 wholesale customers → top **5 = 95% revenue**; chase rest = W4W. Fire two high-drama accounts; stop chasing 95%; clone top profiles → income **$30k→$60k/month**, hours **80→~15** in weeks.

### Parkinson’s Law (paired with 80/20)

**A task swells in perceived importance/complexity with time allotted.** Short deadlines force essentials.

Synergistic pair:

1. Limit tasks to the important → shorter work time (**80/20**).
2. Shorten work time → forces focus on important (**Parkinson**).

**Combined:** identify few mission-critical tasks; schedule with aggressive clear deadlines.

### Daily operating rules

| Rule | Spec |
|---|---|
| Mission-critical items / day | **≤2** |
| Priority test | “If this is the *only* thing I accomplish today, will I be satisfied?” |
| Anti-urgency | What happens if I *don’t*? Worth delaying important? |
| Activity audit (3×/day) | “Am I being **productive** or just **active**?” / “Inventing things to avoid the important?” |
| Multitasking | Forbidden—symptom of task creep |
| To-do capture | Physical constrained list beats infinite digital backlog |
| Macro Parkinson | Take Mon/Fri off or leave by 4pm to force prioritization |
| Heart-attack drills | If only 2 hrs/day or 2 hrs/week possible—what remains? If force-cut 4/5 of time sinks—what survives income? |

**Lack of time = lack of priorities.** Being selective is the productive path.

↔ contra “inbox zero as productivity”: elaborate email efficiency on low-value mail is effectiveness theater.

## ch-6 — Low-information diet {#ch-6}

**Selective ignorance** is practical. Information consumes attention (**Herbert Simon**). Lifestyle design is **output-heavy**; more output requires less input.

**Most information is:** time-consuming, negative, irrelevant to goals, outside your influence—often ≥2 of 4.

### Diet rules (procedure)

- **Just-in-time** info > **just-in-case** info.
- Gate: “Will I **definitely** use this for something **immediate and important**?” No on either → don’t consume.
- **Nonfinishing:** starting doesn’t obligate finishing (article, movie, plate).
- Prefer **how-I-did-it** primary sources + short expert calls over speculative books.
- One-week **media fast** (Band-Aid): no news sites/papers/magazines/nonmusic radio; TV ≤1 hr pleasure; books limited; desk web only for *that day’s* work task; 5-minute lunch “anything important?” via a human—then stop when answers never change actions.

**Bottleneck rule:** problems often solve themselves if you remove yourself as information chokepoint and empower others. “Emergencies” shrink when you don’t answer them.

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Endless industry content, weak shipping | Media fast; only just-in-time inputs |
| Research weeks before decision | Delay deep reading until action deadline approaches |
| Checking news to feel informed | Outsource synthesis to trusted humans; act on rare true signals |

## ch-7 — Interrupting interruption {#ch-7}

**Interruption** = anything preventing start-to-finish completion of a critical task. Three offenders:

1. **Time wasters** — ignorable with little consequence (most meetings, social email).
2. **Time consumers** — necessary but repetitive (email, bills, status).
3. **Empowerment failures** — need approval for small fixes.

### Time wasters: limit access

- Email: off alerts; **batch** (start **2×/day**, e.g. noon + 4pm; move toward **1×** or weekly as system allows). **Never first thing**—finish most important task before ~11am.
- Autoresponder trains world: check windows + urgent phone path only.
- Phone: urgent vs non-urgent numbers; greeting = “I’m in the middle of something—**how can I help you?**” not “How are you?”
- Meetings: prefer email → phone → in-person; require **agenda + questions** before any meeting; cap **≤30 min** with odd end times; meetings are for **decisions on predefined problems**, not defining problems.
- Cubicle defense: headphones / “middle of something” + force 5-second summary → email.
- **Puppy Dog Close:** permanent change via reversible “try once” trial (remote day, no-meeting week).

### Batching (named)

Setup cost often same for 1 unit as 100. **Batch** recurring tasks (email, laundry, bills, reporting). Switching cost can take **~45 minutes** to resume deep work; interruptions consume large fractions of the day.

**Batch economics test:** value your hour → estimate hours saved by longer batch intervals → if cost of “missed emergencies” < value saved, batch further. Real emergencies are rare; most fixes are cheap relative to deep-work recovery.

### Empowerment failures: rules

**$100 fix rule** (start low, raise with evidence): “If it costs less than $X to fix, use judgment—don’t ask me.” Review weekly → monthly → quarterly. First month may cost a bit more; hours returned dominate.

Employees: propose autonomous rules + one-week reversible trial—“Is that reasonable?”

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Inbox runs the day | Batch windows + autoresponder; mission task first |
| Meeting culture with no decisions | Agenda-or-no; 30-min cap or leave |
| You are the $20-decision bottleneck | Written if-then rules + dollar authority for others |
| Recurring chores fragment focus | Batch on calendar; measure fix-cost vs time saved |

## ch-8 — Eliminate → automate → delegate {#ch-8}

**Golden order (Gates magnification + Ferriss):**

1. **Eliminate** undefined/unimportant work.
2. **Automate/streamline** what remains.
3. **Delegate** only what’s left, time-consuming, and **well-defined**.

Never automate inefficiency. Never pay people to do fat you should cut. **Each delegated task must be time-consuming and well-defined.**

**Virtual assistant (VA) as training wheels:** not “need help” justification—**management practice** with small stakes ($100–400 / 2–4 weeks). Goal is learning remote command and replacing yourself. Cost metric = **cost per completed task**, not $/hour (language/back-forth can invert cheap rates). Prefer firms with redundancy over solo hero VAs for critical paths.

**Geoarbitrage quarantine (2007 surface):** cheap India/China hours + live-on-pesos stories are era-bound wage/FX plays. **Transferable mechanism:** price labor in *your* scarce currency (attention/hours), not guilt about DIY; modern stack = AI automation first, then specialized freelancers/APIs, then humans for judgment edge cases.

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Impulse to hire for chaos | Eliminate/systematize first; then hire |
| “I can do it better/cheaper myself” | Compare your fully-loaded hourly rate to theirs × quality |
| Outsourcing without SOPs | Write rules + examples; cap hours; review outputs not activity |

## ch-9 — Finding the muse {#ch-9}

**Muse** (definition): automated vehicle for cash **without consuming time**—not “build a huge company,” not “change the world first,” not IPO-first. Own a business you don’t run.

**Start with the end:** product choice must fit architecture that can run with **≤1 day/week** management after ramp; automatable within **~4 weeks**; testable for **≤$500**. Prefer products over services (constant contact blocks absence)—exceptions rare (e.g. membership without content treadmill).

### Step 1 — Niche first

**Don’t create product then seek market. Find market, then product.** Be a member of the market. Niche: expensive to reach “everyone”; easier/premium to own a tight group with spend and **reachable media**.

Filters: groups you belong(ed) to; specialty magazines/newsletters/communities; **repeat direct-response advertisers** signal profit; full-page cost historically &lt;$5k and readers ≥15k as 2007 proxy—**modern equivalent:** affordable paid reach + clear audience of sufficient size for **your TMI** (see Muse Math idea: validate TAM against dreamline).

**Niche is the new big / target ≠ market:** aspirational demo in creative can sell beyond that demo (iPod ads). Don’t bland-average messaging to “everyone.”

### Step 2 — Product rules (before inventing)

| Constraint | Spec | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Benefit | Encapsulated in **one sentence** | Misunderstood = unsold |
| Price | **$50–200** sweet spot (context-dependent) | Fewer units to TMI; better customers; room for margin |
| Markup target | Aim **8–10×** cost when manufacturing risk exists | Survival buffer when suppliers/ads fail |
| Manufacture lead | **≤3–4 weeks** (prefer 1–2) | Adapt demand; avoid inventory prisons |
| Support load | Fully explainable in strong **FAQ** | Phone FAQ products destroy mobility |
| Category preference | **Information products** for speed/margin/hard-to-clone | vs gadgets tooling + knockoffs |

**Pricing before product / distribution first:** if wholesale needs 40–70% off retail, margins must be designed *before* launch or retail cascade kills you. Prefer **controlled distribution** + MAP-style ad-price discipline over uncontrolled resellers racing to zero.

**Sarah cautionary tale:** shirts sold online then multi-retail without margin architecture → channel conflict, admin load, death. **Ed Byrd pattern:** test demand cheaply (related low-ticket first), premium price, **exclusive** channel, protect margin.

**Expert status (4-week credibility indicators):** trade orgs + summarize top books + free seminars at known institutions/companies + optional trade articles + journalist-query networks. Expert = know more than the *minimal customer base* needs—not global best.

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Idea without reachable audience | Reject; start from niche you inhabit |
| Complex service billed hourly | Productize expertise into downloadable/shippable good |
| Racing to low price | Premium + justify; compete on margin quality of customers |

## ch-10 — Testing the muse {#ch-10}

**Intuition, experience, and focus groups fail.** Ask ten people if they’d buy—then ask them to buy the units in your car. **Only the second answer counts.**

**Micro-testing:** inexpensive ads to measure response **before** manufacturing.

### Transferable three-step (mechanism > 2007 Google surface)

1. **Best:** study top competitors; build a **more compelling offer** on a thin landing page (1–3 pages / 300–600 words): differentiators, testimonials, credibility indicators, risk reversal.
2. **Test:** drive **qualified** paid (or high-intent) traffic for a few days with tight budget; isolate variables (headline, guarantee, price page).
3. **Divest or invest:** kill losers; manufacture/roll winners.

**2007 surface → modern equivalents**

| 2007 tactic | Transferable mechanism | Modern stand-ins (non-exclusive) |
|---|---|---|
| Google AdWords PPC 5 days ≤$500 | Paid demand probe at controlled CPC | Search/social/shopping ads, intent keywords, store-platform boost |
| eBay reserve auction / cancel | Price discovery + real bids | Marketplaces, waitlists with deposit, preorders |
| “Backorder” dry test pages | Intent without inventory (legal/ethical constraints apply) | “Notify me,” waitlist, preorder with clear terms, Wizard-of-Oz fulfillment |
| Print classified + 800 number | Direct-response accountability | Trackable landing + unique codes; community sponsorships with URLs |
| Sign-up as proxy order (e.g. 60% of leads) | Cheap commitment gradient | Email/SMS waitlist, sample content gate, lo-fi sales |

**Quality of traffic:** do **not** bait with “free” if you need buyers—optimize for **qualified** clicks. Specific keywords convert higher and cost less than broad vanity terms.

**Math that survives tools:** track **CPO** (cost per order incl. ads, fulfillment, returns, bad debt), contribution margin, and whether ad spend “sold” enough at target price to cover manufacturing ramp. A $10k product needs far lower conversion than a $50 DVD—judge by **profit**, not vanity CTR.

**Guarantee as lever:** when price page kills conversion, test stronger risk reversal (e.g. aggressive money-back) before cutting price.

**Undecision (preview):** fewer options → more revenue (one-watch ad beat nine-watch **6-to-1**). Limit SKUs, shipping choices, phone orders when scaling.

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Building inventory before demand proof | Micro-test offer first; invent only after paid intent |
| “Friends love the idea” | Require money, preorder, or equivalent costly signal |
| Optimizing brand awareness ads | Prefer fully trackable direct response for muse validation |
| Many product variants at launch | One core offer (+ optional premium); add complexity later |

↔ contra Lean UX / continuous-discovery stacks that assume product teams inside funded orgs: muse testing is **solo cash-flow validation** under hard personal TMI, not feature discovery under OKRs—same scientific spirit, different kill criteria (profit per hour of *your* time).

## ch-11 — MBA: management by absence {#ch-11}

**MBA = Management by Absence.** Design a **process-driven** firm, not founder-driven. Limit contact so managers must solve via rules. You are **not** a tollbooth in the information flow—you’re a roadside officer who intervenes on exceptions via reports.

### Architecture principles

- Prefer **specialist outsourcer companies** over single freelancers for critical functions (redundancy).
- Force outsourcers to **talk to each other**; written authority for inexpensive decisions (start &lt;$100, raise with trust).
- Scalable = 10k orders/week as easy as 10; minimize your decision load.

### Volume phases (shipped units)

| Phase | Action |
|---|---|
| **0–50 total** | DIY all: phone on site, learn FAQ, save CS answers, ship yourself, clarify ads that attract wrong traffic |
| **&gt;10/week** | Publish deep FAQ; add fulfillment that can answer status from your templates; negotiate fees; manufacturer → fulfillment direct |
| **&gt;20/week** | End-to-end fulfillment + processor + optional call center; test hold times; often kill phone orders if online converts |

### Customer filtering (not all customers equal)

Trade as **equals**. After cash flow: 80/20 customers by **profit** and by **time drain**. Put high-maintenance low-profit on autopilot policies (response SLAs, min order, channel rules) or fire them. Prevent bad customers from ordering (qualification, geography, terms).

**Margin Manifesto (selected tenets that remain):**

1. Niche messaging, mass aspiration OK.  
2. What gets measured gets managed (CPO, allowables, LTV).  
3. Pricing/distribution designed together.  
4. Less uncontrolled distribution → more durable profit.  
5. Create end-user demand so you dictate terms; prepaid &gt; net-30 culture for startups.  
6. Good DR advertising works the first time if targeted—don’t buy “7 exposures” folklore for muse tests.  

**Top 13 NR mistakes (ops checklist):** W4W drift; micromanage/email to fill time; solving what others should; re-helping same non-crisis; chasing unqualified customers; answering non-sales email; working where you sleep; skipping 80/20 every 2–4 weeks; perfectionism as W4W; inflating minutiae; manufacturing false urgency; single-project identity; ignoring social life.

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Founder answers every exception | Raise $ authority + if-then docs; review metrics not chats |
| Growth adds drama customers | Filter/fire; optimize profit per owner-hour |
| Too many SKUs/shipping modes | Undecision: cut choices to raise conversion and cut support |

## ch-12–16 — Liberation, mini-retirements, meaning {#ch-12}

**Liberation:** negotiate remote with **results proof** + Puppy Dog trials; employees often need location freedom before hour freedom. Scripts and remote proposals are tactics—mechanism is **measurable output leverage**.

**Mini-retirements:** redistribute recovery and adventure through life (e.g. cadence of intense work projects and relocation/learning blocks), not one terminal retirement. Mobility multiplies purchasing power; quarantine pure 2007 “Thailand on $X/day” tables—update cost-of-living with current data, keep the **relocate to multiply lifestyle output of savings** idea.

**Fill the void:** removing work exposes boredom; plan learning + service *before* full automation. Vague eternal questions fail two gates: (1) terms defined? (2) answer actionable? If not, drop them. Continual learning + service (broadly defined) recur among fulfilled NR.

**Not-to-do list (compressed):** unrecognized numbers; email first/last; meetings without agenda/end; rambling calls; constant inbox; over-serve bad customers; work more when overwhelmed—**prioritize**; 24/7 leash; expect work to fill social void.

## pipeline — DEAL for operators {#pipeline}

```
DEFINE      → NR goals, relative income, excitement, dreamline + TMI/TDI
              fear-set any irreversible-feeling step
ELIMINATE   → 80/20 sources of pain/gain; Parkinson deadlines;
              ≤2 critical tasks/day; low-info diet; batch & refuse
AUTOMATE    → muse rules; micro-test money intent; MBA architecture;
              eliminate → automate → delegate; $ authority rules
LIBERATE    → remote/mobility after (or while) systems hold;
              mini-retirements; fill calendar with learning/service
LOOP        → 80/20 every 2–4 weeks; kill W4W; re-test offers
```

**Employee path:** D → E → **L** → A.  
**Entrepreneur path:** D → E → A → L.

## Anti-patterns

| Trap | Detection cues | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| **Work-for-work’s-sake (W4W)** | Hours as identity; inventing tasks; email as job | 80/20 + dreamline revisit; ≤2 critical tasks |
| **Deferred-life plan** | “After X money/title I’ll live” with rising X | Fear-set; mini-retirements; relative income |
| **Busy-as-virtue** | Always-on, meeting culture, sacrifice theater | Parkinson clocks; batch; refuse |
| **Efficiency cosplay** | Perfect systems for low-value work | Effectiveness first; kill the task |
| **Just-in-case information obesity** | Endless inputs, rare shipping | Media fast; just-in-time gate |
| **Micromanagement loop** | Same exception escalates weekly | Rules + $ authority; stop re-helping |
| **Delegate-the-fat** | Paying humans/AI to do eliminable work | Eliminate before automate/delegate |
| **Build-first muse** | Inventory, patents, perfect product pre-sales | Micro-test money intent |
| **Race-to-bottom distribution** | Any reseller any price | Controlled channels; MAP/exclusivity logic |
| **Option glut** | Huge catalogs, shipping menus, custom everything | Undecision; premium simplicity |
| **Founder tollbooth** | All info routes through you | MBA architecture; reports not chats |
| **Bad-customer worship** | “Customer is always right” with abuse/low margin | Filter equals; fire toxic 20% |
| **Perfectionism-as-procrastination** | 98% quality quests | Good enough on most; great on few |
| **2007 arbitrage cargo-cult** | “Just outsource to India / AdWords prints money” | Keep mechanism; update channels, labor, compliance, ad auctions |

## Applicability & exemptions

**Applies well when:** solo/small operators; info/digital or simple physical products; need personal time freedom; B2C or simple B2B with direct response; can change offers quickly; owner is the bottleneck.

**Weak or wrong fit when:**

- **Safety/regulated products** (drugs, medical claims, aviation, etc.) — FAQ-light muse rules fail; compliance is the product.
- **Deep tech / long R&D** — can’t micro-test full science in 5 days; test *positioning and willingness-to-pay* only.
- **High-touch enterprise / government** — relationship sales and multi-stakeholder deals break “no meetings / no phone” extremes.
- **Marketplace two-sided cold starts** — muse single-SKU logic incomplete (still use demand tests per side).
- **Mission-critical human care** (medicine, therapy, crisis) — absence architecture unethical without coverage design.
- **Employees without remote-possible roles** — L step may be blocked; still use elimination/batching inside role.
- **Teams optimizing for valuation/IPO theater** — muse optimizes owner free cash/time, not growth-at-all-costs.

### Era quarantine (mandatory)

| Quarantined 2007 surface | Keep the mechanism | Do not cargo-cult |
|---|---|---|
| India VA at $4–15/hr as default | Remote management skill; cost-per-task | Assume wage arbitrage always works post-AI/compliance |
| Google AdWords as only validator | Paid intent micro-test | Ignore privacy changes, auction inflation, platform risk |
| Drop-ship CD libraries / magazine cards | Niche + controlled test + margin design | Specific media kits and CPC folklore |
| “Check email Mondays only” as universal | Batch + train expectations | Break SLAs you’ve contractually sold |
| Geoarbitrage price tables | Mobility multiplies purchasing power | Outdated COL numbers; visa/tax naivety |
| Infomercial royalty stacks | Info product leverage | Assume TV DR is easy entry in 2020s |
| E-mail autoresponders as primary CS | FAQ + routing | Spam/legal consent regimes |

**Modern defaults (obvious only):** landing-page + paid intent tests; preorders; no-code stores; AI for first-pass CS drafts with human escalation rules; contractors over “employees everywhere”; measure **profit per owner-hour** continuously.
