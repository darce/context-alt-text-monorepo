## ch-2 — Core thesis: work that is also antiwork; dialectics as decision method {#ch-2}

**Every finished cultural product is a record of people at work** — and often also something else (pleasure, craft, refusal). Berg’s method: take workers seriously when they say both “this is a job / a gig” *and* “fuck overtime / I’d rather not be a laborer.”

**Porno dialectics** (author’s frame): hold contradictions as the meat of the story, not wrinkles to smooth:

| Both true | Decision implication for bootstrap brands |
|---|---|
| Autonomy *and* precarity | Design for exit options, not one boss |
| Pleasure makes work livable *and* extracts more labor | Monetize pleasure; don’t treat it as free fuel |
| Authenticity sustains *and* can be sold off for parts | **Bound** authenticity; price the personal |
| Self-production liberates *and* can make managers of former workers | Prefer trade/solo ownership when possible; name when you hire |
| Gig flexibility is resource *and* risk | Stack income streams; don’t romanticize the stack |

**Politics for the meantime:** most workers want better conditions *now* (own means of production, legibility, less extractive policy) without waiting for pure revolutionary form. Bootstrap brand rule: ship **meantime assets** (owned list, owned files, priced menu) while keeping anti-extractive critique alive.

**Infrapolitics** (James Scott via Berg): everyday loopholes, trade reserves, rate games, and mutual aid count as struggle — not only unions. For brand builders: small hacks that retain ownership compound.

**Key anti-reification rule:** do not treat the product text as the whole story. **Good branding ≠ good work.** “Ethical” aesthetic packaging does not prove fair conditions.

## ch-3 — Zero-capital entry: democratized production and class mobility {#ch-3}

**Historical arc compressed for brand mechanics:**

| Era | Capital barrier | Worker lever |
|---|---|---|
| Golden Age theatrical | High (film/distribution) | Limited self-produce; loops as side door |
| Video | Medium–high | Star contracts + fan clubs / strip circuit |
| Digital / tube piracy | Studio profits collapse; tools cheapen | **Self-produce, own copyright, direct sales** |
| Platform / pandemic | Entry near-zero; market gluts | DTC survivors with existing fan equity win |

**Zero-capital stack** (transferable startup mechanic):

1. **Body / skill / time** as primary capital  
2. **Smartphone + web connection** as production plant  
3. **Trade labor** with peers (share copyright; no cash outlay)  
4. **Scene or public artifact as ad** for owned products  
5. **Fan relationship** as distribution channel  

**Savage axiom (ownership):** *“Not owning your product, you’re in a desperate place. Any time you’re depending on people for your livelihood, you’re in bad shape.”* Design rule: when capital is zero, **the first asset to design is ownership**, not polish.

**Gatekeeping → self-produce:** Black, queer, disabled, fat, and otherwise excluded creators often self-produce because bosses won’t hire them — then discover **better pay and conditions without a boss**, sometimes taking market share from those who excluded them. Niche audiences frequently outpace traditional managers’ taste.

**Democratization cuts both ways:** lower barriers → more creators → **glutted market** → compressed bargaining power *and* wider entry. Bootstrap brands must assume competition floods every open door.

**Manager obsolescence threat** is leverage: it is safer to refuse a boss when you can hire yourself. Identity systems should make **self-hiring** (owned storefront, owned catalog) first-class, not an afterthought.

## ch-4 — Scene as marketing tool; promotional labor stack {#ch-4}

**Dominic Ace (industry publicist):** *“A scene is just a marketing tool.”*

**Promotional labor** (Schieber via Berg): the paid or visible unit is often **loss-leader advertising** for higher-margin owned streams — analogous to a music single after piracy: the track gets you to the tour, merch, and direct relationship.

| Layer | Function | Who captures surplus if you don’t design for it |
|---|---|---|
| Visible unit (scene, post, collab, “gig”) | Attention + legitimacy signal | Platform, studio, host brand |
| Owned catalog (clips, memberships, products) | Recurring/direct revenue | You, if copyright is yours |
| Satellite services (appearances, custom, consulting analog) | High hourly / high intimacy premium | You, if brand transfers |
| Brand residual (name recognition window) | Temporary star equity | Decays ~months without refresh for average creators |

**Star-shelf-life heuristic (interviewees):** average performer carries “star” billing for ~**six months** after last released scene; exceptional brands last longer. Retirement announcements, drip-releases of reserves, and “final” owned products manage the decay curve.

**Wage scenes without residuals:** when you work for a wage/fee with **no royalties**, the rational brand move is to extract **non-wage surplus** — name recognition, B-roll for your feed, wardrobe sold later, fan capture — not to treat the fee as full compensation for lifetime reuse of your image.

**Underwear dialectic** (canonical micro-scenario): producers force performers to buy disposable wardrobe → performers post wish lists so fans gift items → items appear on film (studio gets free wardrobe) → performers **resell worn pieces to fans** (recover cost + margin) → loyalty attaches to **performer name**, not studio.  
**Lesson for zero-capital brands:** when a partner externalizes costs onto you, redesign the cost into a **fan-facing product** that builds *your* equity.

**Strategic under-promotion:** money-focused creators promote **what they own** harder than what a studio owns. Directors who demand free post-shoot promotion for *their* IP are asking for unpaid labor after the check cleared.

## ch-5 — Satellite income streams and multi-client freelancing {#ch-5}

**Core economic fact:** few workers live on primary gigs alone. Income is a **portfolio**.

**Satellite industries** (from porn film’s POV; actually often the real business):

| Stream | Brand role | Constraint notes |
|---|---|---|
| Direct-to-consumer platforms | Primary ownership path | Platform cut, ToS risk |
| Webcam / live interactive | Price-by-minute control | High free-chat tax; emotional labor |
| Appearances / feature gigs | Leverage “star” billing | Hierarchy vs local peers |
| Merch / wish lists / physical goods | Monetize intimacy tokens | Logistics + privacy |
| Custom commissions | High willingness-to-pay | Scope creep without menus |
| Adjacent creative (writing, speaking, toys with royalties) | Longer residual tails | Only if contract owns residual |

**Freelance union shape (Horowitz via Berg):** careers = piecing clients, learning marketing + accounting, offices in bedrooms/cafés. Porn workers had this long before it was named for white-collar freelancers.

**Hustle is not only decline narrative:** racialized and feminized workers have always gig-worked. Many left “secure” jobs **for flexibility**. Decision rule: distinguish **desired multi-stream autonomy** from **forced multi-stream survival** — product design and pacing differ.

**Volatility example:** $20k months to $3.5k months; injury, STI moratoria, agent whims, casting droughts. Brand systems need **cash buffers and non-correlated streams**, not a single peak month as plan.

**Retirement as brand design:** build production company / owned catalog *while* performing; “Hall of Famer + company + brand income” by a planned age. Transferable: design **exit product** before peak attention dies.

**Whorearchy warning (cultural positioning):** communities rank streams by respectability; identifying with the highest-status label can be a **negotiating tool** *and* a solidarity toxin. Bootstrap brands under stigma: don’t climb by stomping adjacent laborers who share your platform risk.

**Sinnamon Love correction (equity of hustle):** ability to refuse low pay depends on **off-platform market power**. White, young, conventional-beauty creators more often have webcam/toy/club alternatives; women of color often cannot refuse. **Diversification is stratified.** Brand advice that says “just raise rates / just refuse” without naming access is false universalism.

## ch-6 — Rights, ownership, trade, and self-production as asset strategy {#ch-6}

**Real asset hierarchy for zero-capital creators:**

1. **Copyright / distribution rights** in what you produce  
2. **Audience relationship** (contact path you control)  
3. **Name/brand** transferable across platforms  
4. **Cash fee** for one-off labor (lowest residual)

**Self-production cost band (era-specific numbers, keep as order-of-magnitude):** bigger projects ~$5k–$20k; smartphone + trade labor → ~$0 cash. Ownership retained; repost across venues.

**Trade shoots:** peer co-production; share labor and **share copyright**. Alternative to director-controlled sets that forfeit profits. Best-practice (Jiz Lee / Siri via Berg): **don’t trade with someone who will profit exponentially more** from the shared scene (asymmetric distribution networks = soft extraction).

**Solo / self-employed surplus:** Gibson-Graham — truly self-employed can **appropriate their own surplus labor**. Design goal: maximize share of catalog where *you* are residual claimant.

**When hiring others:** “a boss is still a boss, even for two hours.” Class mobility is real; so is the ethical load when you become check-writer. Document rates, testing costs, and provisions you hated when you were talent.

**Piracy distinction workers insist on:** pirating studio content ≠ pirating **performer-owned** clips/webcam. #PayForYourPorn targets the second. Brand voice can educate fans: free leaks of *your* files are wage theft, not “exposure.”

**Contract loophole pattern:** company forbids “working” for others → performer banks **unpaid trade** to release after contract ends. Lesson: read exclusivity as **what you may not release**, then design legal reserves.

**Platform middlemen remain:** even “direct” sales often use third parties (fees 10% auction style up to **30–70%** cam splits; ~60% if forced back to extractive cams after free tools ban). Ownership of files ≠ ownership of payment rails.

## ch-7 — Pricing labor: rates, secrecy, hierarchy, menu economics {#ch-7}

**Sara Jay rate rule:** close your eyes — *how much would it take for me to do that?* That is your rate. Below it, you will not feel good. **Maxine Holloway:** knowing price and value sustains careers.

**Money as boundary tool:** clearly defined, **monetized** performances protect boundaries and stave off burnout (contra managers/outsiders who claim money “ruins” authenticity). Underpayment is what workers name as exploitation more often than the work form itself.

**Menu economics (cam example):** itemized prices (“$4 to stand”, “$6 to show X”, exclusive rates) + block threats for non-tippers. Transferable to any creator service: **public menu > vague negotiation**.

**Platform cut transparency:** state the cut (“I only keep 35%”) as part of price defense — educates buyers why “too high” is still low net.

**Rate secrecy is a wage inequality machine:** employers benefit when peers don’t compare; NDAs and culture suppress talk. Brand/community practice: normalize **rate transparency among peers** even when public menus stay strategic.

**Demand-based rate tactic (Charity Bangs):** working too much → raise rate; not enough work → lower. Subject to hierarchy floors: Black and non-normative creators face structural ceilings that “personal brand skill” talk erases.

**Flat-rate “ethical” productions:** equal pay can mean **everyone lower**; may improve consent optics but can reduce high-skill differential pay. Judgment: equalization is not free — know who subsidizes it.

**Art-market vs job framing:** if you treat output as art with fluctuating price, ownership of the final object is what makes fluctuation fair. Wage work without ownership is **not** the same as selling a sculpture you keep title to.

**Manager rate law (Pounder):** lowest rate that still fills the booking. Your counter-power is **exit to self-production + satellite demand**, not moral appeal.

## ch-8 — Authenticity work as brand labor (and how to bound it) {#ch-8}

**Authenticity work** (Peterson; applied by Berg): maintaining the appearance/experience of realness is labor. Fans and managers demand “wanting to be there,” not “just for the money.” Late capitalism’s “do what you love” bites hardest where instrumental motives are stigmatized.

**Dialectical brand rule:** workers are right that loving the work can make it livable; managers are right that love is a tool to pay less. **Design response:** allow real investment *and* put a price and an end time on it.

**Bounded authenticity** (Bernstein via Berg): real-but-circumscribed intimacy — surface acting through deep but timed connection. Brand voice can be warm without being infinitely available.

**Covert mimesis** (Egan via Berg): performing low-power / compliant roles to retain bookings while extracting what you can. High skill, high psychic cost — not a default growth strategy.

**Character vs “just me” (Stoya vs persona split):**

| Strategy | Brand effect | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Full character (voice, birthday, always “on”) | Protects private self | Rigidity; fan entitlement to the mask |
| Strategic breaks / snark / politics | Humanizes; disrupts “perma-amenable” fantasy | Hirability with old bosses; fan backlash |
| “I’m just me” fusion | Coherent lifestyle brand | Life fully put to work |

**Fourth-wall brand:** #realpornawards-style public critique can coexist with career when self-production lowers dependency. Unfiltered voice is a competitive asset *if* you own distribution.

**Anti-pattern:** using “authenticity” as unpaid overtime after the fee is paid (forced promo, forced enthusiasm for bad IP).

## ch-9 — Platform dependency, fees, algorithms, deplatforming {#ch-9}

**Structural platform risks Berg maps:**

| Risk | Mechanism | Brand counter-move |
|---|---|---|
| Extractive fees | 30–70% cuts; “convenience” fees | Dual-home sales; raise prices for cut; prefer lower-cut rails |
| Algorithmic management | Ranking, competition engineering | Diversify discovery; own email/list/off-platform contact |
| ToS whiplash | Silent enforcement, random bans | Export files; multi-platform presence; legal name hygiene |
| Payment processor exile | Banking stigma | Redundant processors; cash-adjacent products |
| Policy enclosure (SESTA/FOSTA era) | 135+ sites shut/restricted; Drive wipes | Assume platforms are **hostile landlords**; own master files offline |
| Fan weaponization | Revenge reports after refusal of free content | Document; paid-link replies; escalate boundaries publicly |

**Enclosure thesis (Federici via Berg):** independent sexual labor threatens capital when workers can live outside the wage; **internet censorship re-separates producers from means of distribution**. Bootstrap brands in any taboo category should treat deplatforming as **expected weather**, not black swan.

**Irony:** censorship sold as fighting “powerful operators” often **re-empowers pimps/managers and high-cut platforms** by destroying DIY advertising and free tools (Zoom/Drive/social) that cut them out.

**Algorithm casting:** directors hire by search/download graphs. Brand implication: **searchable identity + paying-fan signal** is negotiating capital on and off platform.

**Tourists and influencers:** pandemic/platform booms flood markets with stigma-distancing newcomers. Incumbent strategy: deepen **paying** relationship quality, not chase free reach vanity metrics.

**Vanity metric trap:** 100k followers / 10 paying subscribers. **Exposure is not revenue.** Design KPIs around paid conversion and owned contact, not follower counts.

## ch-10 — Hustle critique: what burns people out; rest as cost of goods {#ch-10}

**Life put to work** (Morini/Fumagalli): daily life folded into market. Stoya: *“I’m kind of always working, but it’s also almost always really fun.”* Rare freedom (royalties funding selectivity) vs common pattern (always-on without fun).

**Burnout drivers (falsifiable list):**

1. **Unpaid marketing tax** — constant posting, free chat, free fantasy talk before paid sessions  
2. **Freemium arms race** — free samples that train audiences to expect free labor (can scab peers)  
3. **Self-making costs** — beauty, wardrobe (~$200+/mo cited), meds, travel, tests shifted from employers  
4. **Recalibration unpaid** — healing, infections, stress recovery, surgery, downtime between intense units  
5. **Emotional labor of approachability** while enforcing boundaries  
6. **Volatility + cannot refuse** — taking harmful volume because pipeline is dry  
7. **Managerial internalization** — performer-directors clocking peers’ breaks in dollars  

**Rest is reproductive labor:** time off that only exists to return to work still has a cost. Build **rest into COGS** (price and calendar), not into guilt.

**Free work norms across the economy:** internships, “exposure,” sample shifts. Porn’s difference: promotion can **sometimes** convert to direct pay because class boundaries are porous. That makes free work **harder to refuse**, not ethically free.

**Freemium with intent (Shibari):** some free as trade so paid offers feel fair — *designed* free, not endless free. Rule: every free unit needs a **conversion hypothesis and a kill switch**.

**Stress as occupational health:** Habib — stress about looks, performance, perception may dominate physical risk narratives outsiders prefer. Brand ops: schedule recovery like production.

**Hustle romanticism critique:** “always hustling” as identity exhausts the asset (attention, body, voice). Savage of the hustle: use it to **exit dependency**, not to become the brand of exhaustion.

## ch-11 — Boundaries, safety, and consent as business practice {#ch-11}

**Business-practice frame (not therapy slogans):**

| Practice | Business function |
|---|---|
| Written/menus of what is sold | Scope control; dispute reduction |
| Renegotiation when scope changes | Prevents unpaid scope creep (industry norm: change partner/act → new deal) |
| Block/ban tools | Removes non-paying extractors |
| Testing / safety costs visibility | Either price in or refuse externalization |
| Partner choice in self-produce | Quality + safety as product features |
| Clear start/end of performance | Bounded authenticity; anti-burnout |
| Public refusal of free labor | Sets fan culture; protects peers |

**Consent ≠ desire; consent ≠ enthusiasm:** instrumental, tedious, or unsexy work can still be consensual when terms are met. Outsider “enthusiastic consent only” frames erase working people’s boundary that actually matters: **negotiated terms vs harm**.

**Power is multi-sided:** wage pressure vs star leverage vs walk-away risk of killing a day’s production costs. Brand systems should not pretend equality; they should **document terms**.

**Safety as schedule design:** compressed multi-day shoots without recovery externalize injury onto workers. Self-managed brands: bake recovery days; turn down density that destroys the asset.

**Mutual aid as infrastructure:** peer education on self-production (e.g. BIPOC collective efforts Berg notes) is brand-ecosystem design — teaching ownership is anti-racist economic practice, not only content critique.

**State distrust:** many workers prefer self-organization over regulators that swing between surveillance and neglect. Meantime politics: **privacy + mutual aid + ownership** often beat “please the platform/state to save us.”

## ch-12 — Stigma navigation, mutual aid, and cultural positioning {#ch-12}

**Stigma double bind:** visibility builds brand *and* triggers deplatforming, hiring discrimination in “straight” work, and fan entitlement. Strategy: concentrate visibility where it **pays**, minimize unpaid exposure that only feeds stigma risk.

**Straight jobs as worse default:** many entered because retail/office/creative-precariat paid less and extracted more boredom. Brand positioning for constrained creators: you are not “failing to get a real job”; you are **exiting extractive ones**.

**Do not exceptionalize:** “fallacy of misplaced scale” (Rubin) — sex-work harms are not automatically more serious than other workplace harms. Transferable critique for any stigmatized creator vertical.

**Solidarity breakdowns:** competition for castings; racialized pay gaps; feature dancers vs house dancers; performers who undercut rates. Brand communities need **anti-undercut norms** and rate talk, not only vibe.

**Class as adjective, not noun** (Resnick/Wolff via Berg): morning manager, evening worker. Identity copy should not freeze “founder” or “talent” as moral essences — describe **roles and residual claims**.

**Fuck jobs (epilogue):** naming labor as work is necessary for rent and rights; “one step beyond” refuses to legitimize work as sacred. Brand voice can hold both: **professionalism without work-worship**.

## pipeline — Bootstrap brand decision pipeline {#pipeline}

```
OWN → PRICE → PROMOTE → DIVERSIFY → BOUND → DEFEND → REST → EXIT
```

| Stage | Decision question | Fail state |
|---|---|---|
| **OWN** | Do I hold copyright / transferable name / off-platform contact? | Working only to build someone else’s library |
| **PRICE** | Is the menu public; does it clear my “feel good” floor after platform cut? | Underpay as identity; burnout |
| **PROMOTE** | Does this free unit convert to owned revenue or only landlord metrics? | Exposure theater |
| **DIVERSIFY** | ≥2 non-identical income streams; not all on one ToS? | Single-platform death |
| **BOUND** | Is personal access metered, timed, renegotiated? | Life fully put to work without pay |
| **DEFEND** | Master files offline; redundant rails; stigma-aware comms? | Wipe / ban / revenge report |
| **REST** | Is recalibration costed into calendar and rates? | Asset (body/voice/attention) failure |
| **EXIT** | What residual pays when peak attention ends? | Star-shelf-life cliff |

**Falsifiable gate before saying yes to underpaid “opportunity”:** “If this artifact is pirated or the platform dies tomorrow, what residual do I still hold?” If none, renegotiate ownership or treat fee as **full hazard pay**.

## Applicability & exemptions

**Applies when:**

- Building **personal or micro-studio brands** with little/no outside capital  
- Creator businesses under **stigma, payment discrimination, or platform hostility**  
- Designing **identity systems** where the person *is* the product surface  
- Planning **rights, menus, freemium, and multi-stream** economics  
- Advising **bootstrap startups** that resemble gig portfolios more than VC roadmaps  
- Setting cultural position for **taboo or edge categories** without institutional brand budgets  

**Does not apply / do not over-fire:**

- Engineering UI scales, spacing, shade ramps, WCAG (eng lexicon)  
- Adult content production craft, shooting technique, or erotic direction (out of scope)  
- Institutional brand empires with large capital (use playboy-brand-value for mark/licensing equity instead)  
- Fully waged jobs with genuine residual sharing and benefits — still useful for side-hustle critique, not for “always self-produce”  
- Treating Berg as a growth-hacker manual to extract more unpaid labor from creators  

**Exemptions:**

- **Deliberate short-arc projects** (one campaign, then exit) may skip residual architecture — price hazard fully into the fee  
- **Collective/cooperative brands** may prioritize flat internal rates over personal menu maximization — document the political choice  
- **Trades where law forbids direct sales** need different risk counsel; keep ownership principles, change channels
