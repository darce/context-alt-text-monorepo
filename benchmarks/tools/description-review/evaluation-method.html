Evaluate descriptions against the image and the user's task

Review protocol v1 · Canon v0.26.2 · 29 October 2026. This is an operational review method and a proposed design for future confirmation. It does not change the registered three-model decision rule, reinterpret existing judgments, or establish that any model has won. Human calibration and user-task validation remain required.

1. Define what the description must accomplish

For each image, fix the intended audience, use and required output surfaces before scoring. A title identifies the image; alt text supplies the essential information for the image's purpose; a caption may add supported context. A faithful short alt can be better than a long catalogue. If page context is unavailable, record descriptive adequacy, with accessibility effectiveness still unestablished. Functional and decorative images need their own purpose-specific criteria. This follows W3C WAI's Images Tutorial and [A11Y-02] Alt serves purpose (1.1.1), Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.2, SC 1.1.1.

Use a rubric for open prose. Equivalent wording is acceptable; semantic similarity, BLEU, ROUGE, exact string match and fluent prose do not prove visual truth. A description can be highly similar to a reference while changing a count, negation or identity. [EVAL-11] Open-ended tasks need the right scorer, AI Engineering. The primary research Rohrbach et al., Object Hallucination in Image Captioning (2018) likewise shows that good sentence-metric scores need not mean less hallucination; its object-specific metric is not a complete rubric for attributes, relations, OCR or usefulness.

2. Build the reference independently, then judge each output

  1. Inspect the full image with candidate descriptions hidden. Record essential visible facts, uncertainty, and relevant literal text. Use attributed external context only when that context was available to the model under evaluation.
  2. Keep essential facts distinct from optional detail. The reference is an evidence inventory, not one preferred sentence. Require adjudication for disputed facts; an annotation is not automatically gold.
  3. Read every required output surface. Mark each supported, contradicted, unsupported or unverifiable claim where a claim audit is used. Do not manufacture missing claim labels through token or embedding overlap.
  4. Record issue flags, affected surfaces, severity, OCR state and notes before marking Reviewed. Unknown and uncertain states remain visible. A generation failure stays in the attempted-output denominator but is not labelled a hallucination.

For a future task-specific primary measure, a defensible candidate is the proportion of attempted image outputs judged usable without a material correction: required surfaces present, no material unsupported or wrong facts, no essential omission, and relevant OCR correct or explicitly not applicable. Minor cosmetic edits may be allowed only under a written, calibrated definition. An incomplete review remains pending rather than a pass or a failure. This conjunction is a product-specific proposal to calibrate with humans, not a universal canon formula. The current report's accepted-rate calculation has its own frozen policy, including an omission policy; do not substitute this proposed criterion into it retrospectively.

3. Keep lexicon A/B, model A/B/C and history distinct

  • Lexicon A/B preference: compare the same image, model and extraction arm with writing lexicon OFF versus ON. Choose A, B, Tie, Both unusable or Cannot judge. This estimates lexicon preference within a model; it does not choose the best model.
  • Three-model blind review (A/B/C): use the Preferred description (A/B/C) dropdown above each image to choose Candidate A, B or C, Tie / no preference among all, All unusable or Cannot judge. These are explicit model-preference ballots. Assess factual correctness separately using the rubric. The current E0/W0 comparison has 86 descriptions over 29 images; the one two-candidate image has an A/B dropdown and a separate ranking group.
  • Historical comparisons: Show historical reviews reveals MiMo, Qwen, JoyCaption, Florence and other retained outputs with their saved assessments. Preserve their exact image, output and run identities. Match image bytes, required surfaces, context, prompts, lexicon conditions and model/precision lineage before interpreting a difference. Earlier quantized runs and newer full-precision runs confound model and serving configuration.
  • Keep the 12-image original cohort, 17-image extension, diagnostic subsets, pilots and caption-only references separately labelled. Previously reviewed items are regression evidence, not untouched test data. Do not convert an unknown historical lexicon setting into OFF. Do not pool historical reviews into the registered winner calculation.

Use the same rubric prospectively across comparable candidates, while retaining older annotations under their original rubric. Re-review with a new version when criteria change; never silently rename an old label into new evidence. [PROV-01] Every output walks back to its evidence, Model Cards + AI Engineering.

Export and compare preference rankings

Export JSON preserves raw annotations, lexicon pairs and the optional model_preferences extension. Export ranking JSON also includes the exact description manifest, comparison rule and a reproducible preference_ranking. Re-importing either format preserves the recorded choices. A blank choice stays unscored; changing a choice replaces that image's previous ballot rather than adding a second vote. Candidate order, reviewer, seed, image and output hashes are bound to each comparison.

The ranking orders models by explicit chosen counts within the same candidate roster; equal counts share a rank. The selection rate is chosen ballots divided by all judgeable ballots (preferred, tie or all-unusable). Cannot-judge and unscored images remain separate. The report does not pool the one A/B image with the 28 A/B/C images. This is a descriptive ranking of your preferences, not a calibrated measure of image truth or a population-level winner claim. View Description preference ranking after exiting blind mode, or inspect the exported JSON.

Saved-review comparison and semantic verification checks the two supplied files. operator-reviews.json has 192 historical annotations, 180 marked Reviewed, and 88 lexicon A/B judgments. operator-reviews2.json contains the same 192 output-bound judgment records, with all marked unreviewed and no active preferences. Those files are overlapping snapshots, not 384 independent examples, and neither contains current three-model preferences. The newer unreviewed flags must not silently be treated as confirmation. Raw original files remain unchanged.

Semantic verification compared current descriptions with 42 explicitly preferred historical descriptions for the same images and retrieved supporting canon research. It measures language similarity only. Exact source-key, image-hash, output-hash and surface-text checks establish that all 192 historical annotations refer to the same retained outputs. A similar caption may still contain a wrong fact; the similarity diagnostics do not rank model quality or supply missing human ballots.

4. Measure the judges and control presentation bias

Use independent human review on a calibration subset spanning easy, difficult and safety-critical cases. Keep independent exports separate; the report deliberately refuses conflicting imports. Resolve disagreements through adjudication and preserve the original judgments. Report agreement as reliability, and performance on adjudicated known-answer items as a separate correctness check. One reviewer gives provisional evidence; agreement alone cannot establish truth. [HITL-03] Gold-embedded annotator QC, Human-in-the-Loop ML.

Counterbalance candidate positions, log the presentation mapping, and test some comparisons with swapped positions. Report position effects and check whether verbosity changes judgments. Preserve full outputs for factual scoring; use a separate matched-length sensitivity analysis rather than truncating away errors or essential content. Do not rely on sole same-family model self-scoring. If an LLM judge is introduced, first measure agreement and failure cases against blinded human labels, pin its model/prompt/rubric/decoding/order protocol, and revalidate changes. [EVAL-12] Validate the judge against humans, [EVAL-13] Pin the judging protocol, and [EVAL-14] Counterbalance and measure judge bias, all AI Engineering.

5. Report each model with denominators and uncertainty

For each matched run configuration and image cohort, report attempted outputs, successful outputs, required-surface failures, reviewed outputs, pending and uncertain outputs; then report factual errors, essential omissions, OCR-assessed/error counts, and reference-bound salient coverage. Show per-surface problems and critical slices. Keep correctness, coverage, preference and listening cost separate rather than averaging ordinal labels into an arbitrary score.

Report claim precision only on an explicitly audited set; report the unaudited count. Salient-fact recall uses a reference denominator fixed independently of model output, and is not factual precision. State how coverage was assessed: in the current UI an empty coverage list does not prove a completed zero-coverage audit. Missing and unreviewed cases need explicit bounds or a declared missingness analysis, not exclusion that rewards a failing model.

Compare candidates on the same image IDs. Images are the initial resampling unit, with higher-level clusters when images share a source, subject or scene. Four lexicon conditions, three surfaces and repeated judgments do not turn 29 images into hundreds of independent samples. Use paired differences with cluster-aware confidence intervals; disclose complete-case counts and sensitivity to missing data. [EXP-23] Pseudoreplication check, Statistics Done Wrong, ch. 3.

Before a new confirmatory comparison, freeze the population/sample frame, rubric, primary metric, practical margin, direction of harm, analysis horizon and multiplicity policy. Use fresh held-out images after exploratory model selection. For three models there are three pairwise contrasts; do not treat three unadjusted 95% intervals as a joint 95% claim. If precision is inadequate, report inconclusive rather than equal or a winner. A convenience sample cannot establish production-wide accuracy. [EXP-03] Declare an OEC, Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments, ch. 7; [EXP-12] Power and practical significance, same source, ch. 2; [EXP-10] Primary metric and multiplicity, same source, ch. 3.

Measure speed only after the quality requirements are met, on the same serving profile and attempt denominator. Full-precision quality is not evidence for a quantized deployment. Validate the chosen offline criterion against actual user-task success before treating it as a production release gate. [EVAL-22] Validate the offline proxy against online impact, The ML Test Score, § model-2.

Research basis and boundaries

The research used the pinned published Canon v0.26.2, verified against its release manifest, and read the original private distilled corpus. Local GTE semantic embeddings retrieved relevant source documents; similarity ranked research relevance and never judged whether a model description was true. Retrieved documents were inspected by mechanism, supplemented with direct source inspection and the primary W3C and caption-hallucination sources above. The receipt records model/tokenizer digests, query strings, retrieval results and inspected-source hashes.

Relevant distilled mechanisms include rubric scoring and judge bias in AI Engineering; gold checks, agreement, priming and expert adjudication in Human-in-the-Loop ML; pseudoreplication and multiple comparisons in Statistics Done Wrong; and primary criteria, guardrails and practical significance in Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments. Available original extracts of the latter two were spot-checked. Original book extracts for the first two were not available at their expected corpus paths; their inspected distillations are identified as such.

The canon's signal-density card suggests testing a facts-per-listening-time composite, but explicitly does not establish it as a validated quality metric. Its citation of A11Y-16 does not directly ground a listening-duration budget: that published row concerns user-controlled time limits and motion. Accordingly this method treats listening effort as a product-specific secondary measure and does not cite A11Y-16 as authority for a density score. No automatic truth scorer, human agreement rate, task-success validation or new model ranking has been manufactured.
