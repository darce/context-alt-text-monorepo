## ch-1 — The new rules {#ch-1}

**Core claim:** Humans are **not rational actors**. Kahneman/Tversky: **System 1** (fast, emotional) steers **System 2** (slow, logical). Negotiation that only uses BATNA/ZOPA/win-win scripts fails when the counterpart is in crisis, ego, or fear—i.e., most real deals.

**Tactical empathy** (definition): listening as a martial art—recognize the other side’s feelings and mindset *and* hear what is behind them so you increase influence in moments that follow. Not sympathy (agreeing); not “being nice.”

| Hostage situation | Sales / pricing / partnership translation |
|---|---|
| Kidnapper wants max ransom + status | Buyer wants lowest price + look smart to boss |
| “How am I supposed to do that?” stalls threat | Same line stalls unreasonable discount / scope demand |
| Never split hostages 50/50 | Never split the difference on price “to be fair” |

**Scenario → lesson:** Harvard role-play (Mnookin/Blum): repeated calibrated “How am I supposed to…?” + “How do I know he’s alive?” forces the “kidnapper” to solve *your* logistics. Same move empties a counterpart’s budget reserve in a buyer/seller exercise.

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Deal framed as pure math / BATNA duel | Treat emotion as the main system; logic follows |
| Pressure to “meet in the middle” | Default: **no deal > bad deal**; creative options > split |

↔ contra many Getting-to-Yes popularizations: do **not** separate people from the problem when emotion *is* the problem.

## ch-2 — Be a mirror {#ch-2}

**Assumptions blind; hypotheses guide.** Great negotiators hold multiple hypotheses and test them live. Goal early: **extract and observe**, not persuade.

**Slow. It. Down.** Speed undermines felt listening. Time is a tool; talking ≠ shooting (business: talking ≠ walking).

### Three voice tones

1. **Late-night FM DJ voice** — deep, soft, slow, downward inflection. Authority + calm without dominance. Use for non-negotiables: “We don’t do work-for-hire.”
2. **Positive / playful** (default) — smile while speaking; primes collaboration.
3. **Direct / assertive** — rare; triggers pushback.

### Mirroring

**Mirror** (FBI definition): repeat the **last three words** (or critical 1–3 words) of what they just said. Neuro: we fear difference, bond to similarity. Effect: counterpart elaborates; you buy time; they reveal strategy.

**Confront without confrontation (script sequence):**

1. Late-night FM DJ voice  
2. Start with “I’m sorry …”  
3. Mirror  
4. Silence ≥ **4 seconds**  
5. Repeat  

**Scenario → lesson:** Boss drive-by “Make two paper copies of everything” → “I’m sorry, two copies?” → sequence of mirrors + labels collapses a week of work to “two digital backups.”

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Counterpart dumps an impulsive demand | Mirror last 1–3 words; pause; do not argue yet |
| You feel the urge to fill silence | Wait—silence is their turn to vomit information |
| They overuse “we/they” vs “I/me” | Note status: low self-reference can signal real power or a front |

## ch-3 — Don’t feel their pain, label it {#ch-3}

**Labeling:** validate emotion by naming it. Form almost always:

- “It seems like …”  
- “It sounds like …”  
- “It looks like …”  

Avoid “I” openings (“I’m hearing that…”)—“I” raises guard. After a label: **pause**. Let them correct or expand.

**Mechanism:** labeling moves amygdala activity toward rational regions; negatives lose intensity when spoken.

**Neutralize negatives first, reinforce positives.** Presenting behavior (cranky) ≠ underlying emotion (lonely / scared of misappropriation). Label the underlying driver.

### Accusation audit {#accusation-audit}

Before a hard conversation, list **every terrible thing** they could say about you—and say the worst out loud first (“taking the sting out”).

**Partner / vendor cut example (scripts):**

- “You’re going to think we are a big, bad prime contractor when we are done.”  
- “It seems you feel this work was promised to you from the beginning.”  
- “You may feel like we have treated you unfairly, and that we changed the deal significantly since then. We acknowledge that you believe you were promised this work.”  
- Then open: “What else is there you feel is important to add to this?”  
- On gasp at cuts: “It sounds like you think we are the big, bad prime contractor trying to push out the small business.”

**Business translation:** price increase, SLA cut, missed deadline, pivot that kills a partner’s revenue, layoff announcement, scope renego—audit accusations *before* the meeting.

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Hard news + relationship must survive | Accusation audit → labels → ask them to design the fix |
| Angry customer ahead of you in line / support queue | Label *their* day first; then request |
| You want to deny a fear (“I don’t want this to sound harsh…”) | Don’t deny—label and inflate slightly so they correct down |

**Scenario → lesson:** Sold-out flight: labels + mirrors with gate agent (“It seems like they were pretty upset” / “Booked solid?”) before any ask → Economy Plus open seat.

## ch-4 — Beware “Yes”—master “No” {#ch-4}

**“No” starts the negotiation.** “No” = temporary status-quo protection, not permanent rejection. Real meanings of “No”:

- not ready yet  
- uncomfortable  
- don’t understand  
- can’t afford  
- want something else  
- need more info  
- want to talk to someone else  

Then label / ask:

- “What about this doesn’t work for you?”  
- “What would you need to make it work?”  
- “It seems like there’s something here that bothers you.”  

### Three kinds of “Yes”

| Type | What it is | Business risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Counterfeit** | Escape / keep you talking | Fake pipeline, fake verbal close |
| **Confirmation** | Reflex black/white | No action |
| **Commitment** | Agreement that leads to action | Only this counts |

Pushing early “Yes” trains counterparts to give counterfeit yeses so you leave them alone.

**No-oriented openers (prefer over yes-traps):**

- “Is now a bad time to talk?” > “Do you have a few minutes?”  
- Fund-raising flip: questions designed for “No, things will get worse / No, I won’t sit and watch” raised return **~23%** vs classic yes-ladder  

**Force a “No” when they won’t engage:** intentional mislabel (“So it seems you really are eager to leave your job”) or absurd label (“It seems like you want this project to fail”).

**Email that ends radio silence (verbatim):**

> Have you given up on this project?

Triggers loss aversion + autonomy; usually gets a clarifying “No.”

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Cold outreach / stalled deal | Send the one-line “given up” email |
| Counterpart never says “No” | Treat as indecisive, confused, or hidden agenda—walk |
| Telemarketer / hard-close script energy in your own pitch | Rewrite for “No”-safe autonomy |

## ch-5 — Trigger “That’s right” {#ch-5}

**Behavioral Change Stairway (BCSM):** active listening → empathy → rapport → influence → behavioral change. Real change needs **unconditional positive regard** (Rogers), not compliance theater.

**Two words that transform:** **“That’s right.”**  
**Disaster words:** **“You’re right.”** (“You’re right” = shut up and leave me alone; no ownership.)

### How to trigger “That’s right”

Build a **summary** = **paraphrase + labeling** of “the world according to them.” Full, accurate reflection of their story and emotions. Their free-will endorsement: “That’s right.”

Active-listening toolkit for a breakthrough reset:

1. **Effective pauses**  
2. **Minimal encouragers** (“Yes,” “OK,” “Uh-huh,” “I see”)  
3. **Mirroring**  
4. **Labeling**  
5. **Paraphrase**  
6. **Summarize** (paraphrase + label)

**Sales translation:** don’t pitch features first. Reflect the buyer’s passion and constraints until “That’s right,” *then* map product to their world.

**Scenario → lesson:** Pharma rep: “You seemed very passionate about treating [patients]…” → “That’s right” → doctor volunteers trial. Ex-boss job move: summarize his real need (HQ ally / VP track) → two “That’s right”s unlock transfer.

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Deadlock on framing / “principles” money | Full summary of their narrative until “That’s right” |
| They say “You’re right” | You have not landed—re-summarize until they own it |
| You want behavior change (process, pricing, habit) | Aim for “That’s right,” not “Yes” |

## ch-6 — Bend their reality {#ch-6}

**Never split the difference.** Compromise is face-saving, not value-creating (black shoe + brown shoe). **No deal is better than a bad deal.**

### Deadlines

- Deadlines create impulsive, self-harming concessions.  
- Most corporate “hard” deadlines are flexible; research (Moore): **reveal your deadline** → fewer impasses, better deals. Hiding yours makes *you* concede faster while they hold out.  
- Use *their* calendar: end of month (car dealers), end of quarter (sales quotas), Friday party-money dynamics (Haiti pattern → lower settlements late week).  
- Threat specificity ≈ realness: count What / Who / When / How in threats.

### The F-word: “Fair”

Three uses:

1. **Defensive judo** — “We just want what’s fair” → triggers concession. Response: “Okay, I apologize. Let’s stop and go back to where I started treating you unfairly and we’ll fix it.”  
2. **Attack** — “We’ve given you a fair offer.” Response: mirror “Fair?” + label “It seems like you’re ready to provide the evidence that supports that.”  
3. **Constructive** (early): “I want you to feel like you are being treated fairly at all times. So please stop me at any time if you feel I’m being unfair, and we’ll address it.”

### Prospect theory tools (price & offer design)

| Tool | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Anchor emotions first** | Accusation-audit the bad news *before* the number (“By the time we hang up you’ll think I’m a lousy planner… Still, I wanted you to see this before someone else”) |
| **Let them name money first** (default) | Avoid winner’s curse; prepare to take an extreme punch |
| **Range / bolstering range** | “People in this role at X get $130k–$170k” (low end = what you want) |
| **Pivot to nonmonetary terms** | Mag cover, title, timing, support—cheap to them, dear to you |
| **Odd numbers** | $37,263 feels calculated; round 0s feel movable |
| **Surprise gift** after extreme anchor | Reciprocity after rejection of extreme |

**Loss aversion rule:** not enough to show gain; they must feel **concrete loss if deal dies**.

**Salary pipeline (business):** (1) pleasantly persistent on nonsalary terms; (2) define success metrics for next raise with boss; (3) ask “What does it take to be successful here?” → unofficial mentor stake.

## ch-7 — Calibrated questions {#ch-7}

**Calibrated question:** open-ended query the other side can answer but that has **no fixed yes/no**; buys time; gives **illusion of control** while you frame the path.

### Design rules

- Prefer starts with **What** and **How** (sometimes Why only when defensiveness serves *your* goal).  
- Avoid closed stems: can / is / are / do / does.  
- “Why” is accusatory in any language—use sparingly, e.g. “Why would you ever change from your long-standing vendor and choose our company?”  
- Soften confrontations: “You’re screwing me” → **“How am I supposed to do that?”**

### Standby calibrated questions (verbatim keepers)

- “What is the biggest challenge you face?”  
- “What about this is important to you?”  
- “How can I help to make this better for us?”  
- “How would you like me to proceed?”  
- “What is it that brought us into this situation?”  
- “How can we solve this problem?”  
- “What’s the objective? / What are we trying to accomplish here?”  
- **“How am I supposed to do that?”**  
- “When you originally approved this, what did you have in mind?”  
- “What do you hope to achieve by [leaving / walking]?”  

**Self-control gate:** without emotional regulation, scripts fail (~90% when calm; ~0% when you vent). Bite tongue; Japanese translator pause pattern; on attack → calibrated question, not counterattack.

**Behind-the-table always:** there is a team. Influence implementers, not only the speaker.

| Observable situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Unreasonable demand | “How am I supposed to do that?” (deferential tone) |
| Showdown / firefight dynamic | Convert command to calibrated question; suspend “unbelief” |
| You feel “hostage mentality” rising | Pause; apologize if needed; re-ask How/What |

## ch-8 — Guarantee execution {#ch-8}

**“Yes” is nothing without “How.”** Success = implementation (signed check, shipped work, released hostage), not verbal agreement.

### How questions as soft “No”

- “How can we raise that much?”  
- “How can I do that when I have to sell my cars and trucks?”  
- “I’d love to help, but how am I supposed to do that?” (unpaid invoice → client pays)

**Implementation pair (push to their voice):**

- “How will we know we’re on track?”  
- “How will we address things if we find we’re off track?”  

Summarize until **“That’s right.”**  
Beware **“You’re right”** and **“I’ll try”** (= plan to fail)—dive back into How questions.

### Behind the table / Level II players

Ask: “How does this affect the rest of your team?” / “How on board are the people not on this call?” / “What do your colleagues see as their main challenges?” One bit player can kill a CEO-approved deal.

### 7-38-55 percent rule (Mehrabian)

Message impact when forming attitudes: **~7% words, ~38% tone, ~55% body/face**. Use for congruence checks—not as a “pricing formula.” If words ≠ tone/body → label: “I heard you say ‘Yes,’ but it seemed like there was hesitation in your voice.”

*(User note: “7-37-93” is a common misremember; canonical Voss text is **7-38-55**. Pricing anchors live in ch-6/ch-9 Ackerman.)*

### Rule of Three

Get agreement to the **same commitment three times** in one conversation (varied tactics: yes → “That’s right” summary → How implementation question). Hard to fake thrice.

### Liars & status tells

- **Pinocchio Effect:** more words, more third-person pronouns, more complex sentences when lying.  
- Heavy **I/me/my** → often *less* decision power; scarcity of first person → often *more* power (keeps options open).  
- **Chris discount:** use *your* name playfully to humanize (“What’s the Chris discount?”).

### Multi-step “No” (bid against themselves)

Express “No” up to **four times** before the word:

1. “How am I supposed to do that?”  
2. “Your offer is very generous, I’m sorry, that just doesn’t work for me.”  
3. “I’m sorry but I’m afraid I just can’t do that.”  
4. “I’m sorry, no.” (downward inflection)  
5. Plain “No” only if needed.

## ch-9 — Bargain hard {#ch-9}

### Three negotiator types (treat them as *they* need)

| Type | Time = | Silence means | Approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Analyst** | preparation | thinking | Data, no surprises, smile, give think-time |
| **Accommodator** | relationship | anger (sometimes) | Friendly + force implementation questions; surface hidden objections |
| **Assertive** | money | invitation to talk more | Mirror/label until they feel heard; then “That’s right” |

**Black Swan rule (anti–Golden Rule):** treat others the way **they** need to be treated, not how you want to be treated.

### Taking a punch

Expect extreme anchors. Deflect: “How am I supposed to accept that?” / “What are we trying to accomplish here?” / pivot to terms: “Let’s put price off to the side… what would make this a good deal?” Any response **not** outright rejection of your offer → you have the edge.

### Ackerman bargaining (verbatim ladder)

1. Set **target price** (goal).  
2. First offer at **65%** of target.  
3. Three raises of decreasing increments: **85% → 95% → 100%**.  
4. Heavy empathy + many ways of saying “No” so **they counter before you raise**.  
5. Final amount: **precise nonround** number (e.g. $37,893).  
6. On final number, throw a **nonmonetary item they probably don’t want** (signals limit).

Psychology baked in: extreme anchor, reciprocity, diminishing concessions juice their self-esteem, odd numbers feel final.

**Punch-back tools (last resort):** real controlled anger at the *proposal* (“I don’t see how that would ever work”); threats without anger with poise (“I’m sorry that just doesn’t work for me”); **I-messages**; walk-away readiness. Person ≠ problem.

## ch-10 — Find the Black Swan {#ch-10}

**Black Swan (negotiation sense):** unknown unknown—information neither side fully values that, if revealed, changes everything. Voss heuristic: **each side holds at least 3–5** such pieces. Company name: The Black Swan Group.

**Mindset:** known knowns guide but must not blind (Griffin bank: “no one kills on deadline”—until someone did). Beginner’s mind; ask “Why are they saying *this* right now?”

### Three types of leverage

| Leverage | Definition | Business use |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | Ability to give/withhold what they want | Competition among buyers; staged release of deliverables |
| **Negative** | Ability to inflict pain/loss | Use sparingly; prefer labels (“It seems like you don’t care what position that leaves me in”) over threats |
| **Normative** | Their own norms/standards against inconsistency | “You said you pay X multiple—here’s that math” |

### Hunting methods

- **Know their religion** (worldview, not only theology)—frame demands inside it (Watson “Dawn of the Third Day”).  
- **Similarity principle** — shared identity → trust (“stewardship” with Christian CEO).  
- **Hopes and dreams** — sell a map to their unattained goals.  
- **“Because”** works; better when reason references *their* religion.  
- Face time > email for Black Swans; unguarded edges of meetings.  
- When it “doesn’t make sense,” **there’s cents to be made**—label the irrationality; probe constraints.

### “They’re crazy” is a clue (Malhotra/Bazerman via Voss)

1. **Ill-informed** — supply missing data.  
2. **Constrained** — legal, precedent, no authority (classic non-reply after perfect email).  
3. **Other interests** — calendar, fairness vs peers, secret buyer, status.

**Scenario → lesson:** Charleston property: label “cash cow seller must doubt market” → broker reveals seller needs cash for other mortgages → extreme anchor works; timing Black Swan.

## Pipeline — negotiation as procedure {#pipeline}

Operational sequence for sales / pricing / partnership:

1. **Prep One Sheet:** goals, worst accusations, labels, calibrated questions, Ackerman numbers, walk-away.  
2. **Voice + mirror** to open discovery; slow down.  
3. **Accusation audit** + labels to clear fear.  
4. **Invite “No”**; hunt real objections.  
5. **Summarize to “That’s right.”**  
6. **Calibrated How/What** to shape their solution = your solution.  
7. **Numbers:** emotion anchor → let them go first when uninformed → Ackerman if haggle → odd finals + nonmonetary sweetener.  
8. **Rule of Three + How implementation** with behind-the-table stakeholders.  
9. **Black Swan scan** when stuck: constraints, third parties, incongruence (7-38-55).  
10. **Execute** or walk—no split-the-difference face-saves.

## Anti-patterns

| Trap | Detection cue | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes addiction** | Script ladders of trivial yeses; fake pipeline | Design for early safe “No” |
| **You’re-right theater** | Nodding agreement, zero behavior change | Re-summarize to “That’s right” |
| **Split-the-difference habit** | “Let’s just meet in the middle” as moral default | Reframe to creative packages or walk |
| **Speed closing** | Self-imposed fake deadlines driving bad terms | Patience; exploit *their* real calendars |
| **Fair bomb sucker-punch** | Immediate concession after “fair” | Mirror + demand evidence / reset |
| **Why-interrogation** | Counterpart goes defensive | Switch to What/How |
| **I’ll-try close** | Soft commitment language | How implementation until concrete plan |
| **CEO-only sell** | Deal dies after “full buy-in” | Always probe Level II stakeholders |
| **They’re crazy” exit** | You stop listening | Treat as ill-informed / constrained / other interests |
| **Anger cosplay** | Faked rage for leverage | Backfires; only controlled real umbrage at proposals |
| **Round-number anchors** | $50k / $100k placeholders | Odd calculated figures |
| **Email-only Black Swan hunt** | Long threads, no face time | Get offline for unguarded truth |

## Applicability & exemptions

**Applies strongly when:** human decision-makers; asymmetric info; relationship + price both matter; enterprise sales, fundraising, salary, vendor renego, partnership restructuring, support escalations.

**Weaken / adapt when:**

- Fully automated markets / RFPs with no human discretion (procedure still helps edge cases, not the auction math).  
- One-shot pure commodity reverse auctions where relationship value ≈ 0 (still: odd numbers, deadlines, walk-away).  
- Legal discovery / regulated disclosures that forbid certain labels or “illusion” framings—keep empathy; drop manipulative mislabels.  
- True life-safety crises with command protocols (book’s origin domain)—use trained teams, not solo sales scripts.

**Era-correction:**

- **Hostage / 1990s–2000s FBI cases** are war-story packaging; mechanisms (autonomy, loss aversion, summary-triggered ownership) transfer.  
- **Phone-first cold outreach** examples → same “No”-oriented and “given up” patterns work on email/LinkedIn/Slack; tone harder in text—compensate with labels and shorter asks.  
- **7-38-55** is Mehrabian attitude research, often over-generalized; use as **congruence heuristic**, not a scientific price weight.  
- **Car-lot / outlet-mall** anecdotes → modern: SaaS annual contracts, marketplace fees, cloud commits—same Ackerman + nonmonetary pivots (seats, support tier, case study rights, roadmap influence).  
- Cultural note: Voss claims “given up” email worked in Arabic/Chinese contexts famous for avoiding “No”—still test locally; autonomy dynamics are portable, face-saving norms differ.

**Ethical floor:** tools can coerce. Prefer information-seeking tactical empathy over humiliation. “No deal > bad deal” protects long-term reputation (sharks eventually find empty pipelines).
